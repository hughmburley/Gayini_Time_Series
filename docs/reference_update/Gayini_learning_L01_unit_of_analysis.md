# L-01 — The management zone is not an ecological unit

**Logged:** 28 July 2026 · **Source:** T10 Gate C decomposition, and the pilot scan in
`Gayini_locating_results_in_country_note.md`
**Class:** analytical design · **Affects:** every paddock-grain number in the project

---

## The learning

Management zones were drawn for grazing rotation. They do not follow vegetation boundaries,
so a number computed at paddock grain averages across whatever ecological contexts the fence
happens to enclose. Where a paddock spans several communities, that average describes no real
place.

Bala 29ca is the extreme case — Inland 35%, Riverine 33%, Aeolian 32% — and its parts behave
oppositely: two are the lowest and second-lowest of their kind on the property and recovering
strongly, the third is unremarkable and drifting down with everything else. The paddock-level
floor of 40.5% and trend of +0.682 pp/yr are both means over that, and both conceal it.

**It is not a rare problem.** Of 64 paddocks:

| dominance of largest community | paddocks |
|---|---|
| single community (100%) | 25 |
| 90–100% | 17 |
| 75–90% | 8 |
| 60–75% | 5 |
| **below 60% — genuinely mixed** | **9** |

Fourteen paddocks are below 75% dominance. Nine are genuinely mixed: Bala 29ca, Dinan 3,
Mara 8, Dinan 8, Mara 13, Mara 2, Dinan 11, Dinan 13, Bala 6.

## The corollary

Spatial structure in ecological response is expected, not a discovery. The finding is not
that recovery is geographically organised — it is that **an analysis aggregated to
administrative units cannot see geography at all**, and will attribute to management whatever
the fence lines happened to enclose. Keeping the analysis spatially explicit is the robust
default, not a refinement.

The demonstration: Dinan 10's whole-paddock water-adjusted trend is +0.020 pp/yr, rank 55 of
64, indistinguishable from nothing. Its Riverine part is +0.737, among the strongest on the
property. The paddock average erased it.

## What this reaches

**PIN 2 needs revisiting after 10 August.** I pinned paddock grain over paddock × community for
headline number #1 on the grounds that "the claim's unit is the paddock." That was a rhetorical
argument, not an ecological one — I matched the grain to how the sentence was worded rather than
to what the sentence was about. The pinned value (−13.1) remains defensible and its spread
(−9.1 to −14.8) is recorded, so nothing is broken. But the reasoning is weak and the decision
should be re-taken, or the claim restated at part grain. Add to `dim_headline_number`
`decision_note` when reopened.

**T1, T6 and the T2 community-grain tables are unaffected** — all are computed at stratum or
community grain by construction, which is why T6's within-stratum contrasts have been the most
stable results in the project.

**The 21 paddock reports are exposed.** They report paddock-level numbers to a client audience.
For the fourteen paddocks below 75% dominance the headline figure describes an average of
unlike country. At minimum those reports should state the paddock's composition; better, they
should report by part. This belongs to the report stream, not here.

**The deck's slides 7 and 8 are paddock grain.** Being rebuilt after 10 August anyway.

## Standing consequence

Default to the finest grain the support allows, and aggregate upward only with the composition
stated. Where a unit spans contexts, report the parts before the whole — and if only one number
can be given, say what it is an average of.
