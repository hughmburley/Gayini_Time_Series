## Section text

### 11. Spatially structured vegetation response

Management zones were drawn for grazing rotation. They follow stock routes, water points and
fences, and they do not follow vegetation boundaries. A number computed at paddock grain therefore
averages across whatever ecological contexts a fence happens to enclose, and where a paddock spans
several communities that average describes no real place.

This is not a caveat on one result. It bears on every paddock-grain number in this assessment,
including the sixty grazed paddocks that carry no reference-state interpretation at all.

#### 11.1 How often it matters

Of the 64 management zones, 25 lie wholly within a single vegetation community, and for those the
paddock average is an honest description. ⚠ Seventeen more are between 90 and 100 per cent a single
community, eight are between 75 and 90, five between 60 and 75, and **nine fall below 60 per cent —
genuinely mixed country under one name**. Fourteen paddocks sit below 75 per cent dominance.

Figure 19 shows the consequence at property scale: within-paddock differences between parts have a
median of approximately 13 percentage points and reach 40.

#### 11.2 Three paddocks, three outcomes

The named set here is **Bala 27ca, Bala 29ca and Dinan 10**. A different three-paddock set appears
in Section 10 and shares no members with it; neither is referred to by count alone.

**Bala 27ca is entirely Inland Floodplain.** Its whole-paddock figures describe one kind of country
and require no decomposition. Roughly two in five paddocks on this property are of this kind, and
for them the paddock average is the right unit.

**Bala 29ca is the extreme case** — Inland 34.6 per cent, Riverine 33.1, Aeolian 32.3, the most
community-mixed paddock of the 64. Its parts behave in opposite directions. ⚠ Its Aeolian third
sits 32 percentage points below its community's property median and is the lowest of 17 comparable
parts; its Riverine third sits 25 below and is the second lowest of 37; its Inland third sits 6
below and is unremarkable. ⚠ The paddock-level cover floor of 40.5 per cent is a mean over 29, 67
and 35, and the paddock-level trend of +0.682 percentage points a year is a mean over +0.560,
−0.216 and +0.564. Both numbers are true and neither describes a place.

**Dinan 10 is the case that matters most, because it is grazed.** ⚠ Its whole-paddock
water-adjusted trend is +0.020 percentage points a year, ranking 55th of 64 — indistinguishable
from no change. ⚠ Its Riverine part is +0.737, among the strongest on the property. That part is 59
hectares of 841. A real recovery on seven per cent of a paddock, averaged with 782 hectares of
persistently poor ground, is reported as nothing happening.

#### 11.3 What follows from this

Spatial structure in ecological response is expected rather than surprising. The finding here is
narrower and more uncomfortable: **an analysis aggregated to administrative units cannot see
geography at all, and will attribute to management whatever the fence lines happened to enclose.**

Keeping the analysis spatially explicit is therefore the robust default, not a refinement. Where
this assessment reports at part grain it is not being more precise than necessary; it is avoiding an
artefact of the mapping.

The corollary runs the other way as well. The stratum-grain and community-grain results of Sections
7 and 10 are computed on ecological units by construction, and they have been the most stable
results in this project — the within-stratum contrasts of Figure 26 in particular. Stability under
re-cutting is not a coincidence; it follows from the unit being an ecological one to begin with.

#### 11.4 What this assessment has not resolved

⚠ The headline conserved-versus-grazed difference is registered at paddock grain rather than at
paddock-by-community grain. The pinned value is defensible and its spread across grains is
recorded, but the grain was chosen to match how the claim was worded rather than what the claim was
about. **That decision should be re-taken, or the claim restated at part grain.** It is recorded
here rather than quietly corrected.

The unit reports carry paddock-level figures to a client audience. For the fourteen paddocks below
75 per cent dominance, the headline figure in those reports is an average across country that is not
alike. **The report producer already handles this**: a paddock spanning more than one community
receives a dedicated parts page setting out each part against comparable country elsewhere, and a
single-community paddock does not. The rule in this section is therefore already implemented in the
outputs; this section states it.

## The figure — new Figure 21, or 18 if the cuts are applied

### What it draws

Three rows, one paddock each, in the row-per-unit grammar of the rebuilt Figure 21.

**Reuse the parts-page producer from the report stream.** It already draws exactly this: grey dots
for every part of that community property-wide, coloured diamonds for this paddock's parts, and a
vertical line for what is typical for that country. Building a second, separate design would make
the methods document and the reports look like two arguments instead of one.

| Row | Paddock | What the reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bala 27ca** | a single diamond on the vertical line — one community, whole and part coincide |
| 2 | **Bala 29ca** | three diamonds spread far apart, two at the extreme low end |
| 3 | **Dinan 10** | three diamonds all low, with the whole-paddock marker sitting among them and describing none |

**Add to each row a distinct whole-paddock marker** — the report's parts table gives the whole as a
row but the chart does not draw it, and the whole-versus-parts contrast is this figure's entire
point. Open circle, same axis.

**Print each part's area share.** Dinan 10's Riverine part being 59 ha of 841 is the argument; a
diamond alone cannot carry it.
