## 4 · Stage A — how far spatial structure reaches

### Ranges, spherical, isotropic, median of ten seeds

| community | range | nugget | partial sill | ten-seed spread | §4.4 branch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Aeolian Chenopod | **1,110 m** | 50.3 | 102.1 | 1,069–1,172 m | **branch 2** (250 m – 2 km) |

| Inland Floodplain | **3,217 m** | 43.7 | 63.0 | 2,892–3,779 m | **branch 3** (> 2 km) |

**Structure reaches kilometres, not metres.** That settles the question §1 posed.

### The finding that qualifies all of it — two of three are not stationary

The ten-seed check passed on all three. **It measures sampling stability and says nothing
about whether the model form fits**, and the model form does not fit two of the three:

| community | pseudo-R² | RMSE as % of sill | empirical shape |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Aeolian Chenopod | **+0.26** | 12.8% | **turns over** — peaks at 7.9 km, falls **39.5%** by 20 km |
| Riverine Chenopod | **+0.55** | 10.9% | **turns over** — peaks at 4.9 km, falls **22.5%** |
| Inland Floodplain | **+0.78** | 5.9% | plateaus (falls 3.4%) — the model describes it |

A variogram that rises and falls is not a stationary field reaching a sill; it carries
large-scale structure of its own. **For Aeolian and Riverine the fitted range summarises a
shape the data does not have**, and every number derived from it inherits that. This is
carried as a column in `SPAT1_variogram_fit_quality.csv`, in the caveat of every pinned
number, and on the face of F1. **Not specified in the spec — added because the stability
check alone would have licensed three numbers of which only one is well founded.**

### Anisotropy (§4.3) — strong, and not averaged away

Spherical range by direction, 0° = east–west:

| community | 0° | 45° | 90° | 135° | max/min | longest |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Aeolian | **5,766 m** | 681 | 753 | 1,767 | **8.5** | east–west |
| Riverine | 4,122 | 2,866 | 3,064 | 3,329 | 1.4 | east–west |
| Inland | 7,343 | **unresolved** | 1,658 | 2,958 | — | north-east |

**Inland's north-east direction had not decayed within the 20 km measured.** Under Ruling
EN that is **not a range**: the fit returned 37.7 km, which is an extrapolation past the
binned lag, so the number is flagged, withheld, and drawn as an open bar at the measurement
limit. What it licenses is the statement that structure persists beyond 20 km along that
axis — which is itself the strongest anisotropy result here.

This is a floodplain and water moves along paths. An isotropic average would understate
reach along the flow and overstate it across, so the four directions are kept apart.

### Effective sample size (§4.4)

Clifford, Richardson & Hémon (1989), *Biometrics* 45:123–134, in the variance-inflation
form `n_eff = n / (1 + (n−1)·ρ̄)`, with ρ̄ the mean fitted correlation over all pairs in the
set.

| unit set | community | n | n_eff | ratio |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **pixel census** | Aeolian | 77,544 | **159.7** | 0.0021 |
| **pixel census** | Riverine | 193,658 | **74.0** | 0.0004 |
| **pixel census** | Inland | 717,627 | **170.2** | 0.0002 |
| paddock × community areas | all | 100 | **74.9** | 0.749 |
| unzoned tracts | all | 39 | **34.6** | 0.887 |
| paddocks | all | 64 | **59.1** | 0.923 |

**The two assertions this project has been making are both correct, and by wildly different
amounts.**

- **At pixel grain the collapse is total.** 717,627 Inland cells carry the information of
  about **170**. An interval computed on the nominal n would be too narrow by a factor of
  **65**. Every "~1M pixels is not independent n" caveat in the project understated the
  problem rather than overstating it.
- **At unit grain the deflation is modest.** 100 parts behave like **75**; 64 paddocks like
  **59**. Intervals need widening by roughly **10–16%**, not reworking.

**The mechanism, because the numbers look strange side by side.** `n_eff` saturates at
`1/ρ̄`: past a point more cells buy no more independent information. The property is ~55 km
across and the range is ~3 km, so most pairs are uncorrelated and ρ̄ is tiny — which caps
the pixel census near 170 however many cells it has, while 100 units are already few enough
that the same ρ̄ barely bites.

**Every one of these thirteen numbers is pinned** in `dim_headline_number` as
`spat1_n_eff_*` (§4.4.1), each caveat carrying the method by name, the fitted range, the
model form, the ten-seed spread, the maximum lag, the isotropy assumption **and the model
adequacy**. `SPAT1_effective_n.csv` carries each row's `number_id`. Any interval computed
from them cites the `number_id` at the point of quotation (CZ).

## 5 · Overrides

**O1 · Model adequacy added to Stage A, not in the spec.** §4.2 specifies a ten-seed
stability check, which passed on all three communities. Stability is about sampling; it
cannot see that a spherical model is the wrong shape. Without the adequacy measure this run
would have reported three ranges of which only one is well founded, all looking equally
solid. Same family as *a check that fires is not a check that found something*.

**O2 · Ruling EN enforced as a column, not a caption.** The unresolved Inland 45° fit is
flagged in `SPAT1_variogram_models.csv`, so a later reader cannot lift 37,672 m out of the
table. Correcting the caption would have left the number in the file (ER).
