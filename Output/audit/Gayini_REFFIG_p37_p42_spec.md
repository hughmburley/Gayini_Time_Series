### The three claims, kept separate

Every caption on these pages must stay on the right side of this line.

1. **The categories do not separate on this metric.** Descriptive. Supported. Say it.
2. **The design cannot distinguish them.** Four against sixty, confounded with water, one paddock holding the dry end, no stocking data, no cultivation history. A statement about the study. Supported, stronger, say it — but as its own sentence, not as a conclusion drawn from the figure.
3. **Grazing does not affect ground cover.** Not supported. No caption may imply it, and phrases of the form "on this evidence, X does not..." invite exactly that reading.

## 2. Render specification

### 2.1 Layout

Three community panels **stacked one per row**, not side by side.

- **Shared x-axis**, drawn once beneath the bottom panel, water years 1988–89 to 2022–23.
- **Common y-axis across all three panels.** Already satisfied in the current render — the three panels share one scale and one axis label. Keep it, and state on the figure that the scale is common, which the current render does not say.
- Flood-year shading repeated in every panel. It is the same water in all three, and drawing it three times is the point rather than redundancy.
- Community name inside each panel, top left, not as a strip label.

### 2.2 Canvas — this is the blocking item

Figures 3 through 28 currently sit inside a single landscape section: usable text box approximately **10.30 × 7.02 in**. Stacking three panels there yields ~1.7 in per row, which is worse than the present three-across layout.

The portrait sections give approximately **6.77 × 10.19 in**.

**Target:** portrait, figure ~6.7 in wide × ~8.6 in tall, leaving ~0.8 in for the numbered caption and the surrounding text hook. Each panel then runs ~2.6 in tall by ~6.7 in wide.

The gain is horizontal, and it is the justification: **~0.19 in per water year against ~0.086 in now.** A 35-point series needs the width more than it needs the height.

**This requires a section break in the document generator as well as a device change in the producer.** Neither is authorised by this spec. Figures 26 and 27 take the same treatment.

### 2.3 Line treatment

The whole finding is that one of the four behaves differently from the other three. The drawing must carry that.

- **Bala 29ca:** heaviest weight, fully saturated.
- **The other three conserved paddocks:** lighter weight, same colour family, visually a group.
- **Direct labels at the right edge**, not a legend. The stacked layout has room and a legend costs a lookup on every panel.
- **Print n on every panel** — the number of grazed units contributing to the band in that community. See §4.

### 2.4 Vocabulary

Replace **"reference"** wherever it appears as an arm or series label, in both `T2_gateE_figures.R` and `T6_gateE_figures.R`. The document's own finding is that these four do not function as a reference set; a label asserting otherwise, explained away in the caption, is worse than a neutral label.

Use **"conserved"** — it matches the pack and the deliverables register. The methods document currently says "ungrazed" throughout while the pack says "conserved"; a reader receiving both gets two names for one set of four paddocks. Settle on one before the tenth.

## 3. Replacement text

Slots marked **[PENDING-n]** are held until CC confirms. Do not ship with a slot filled by assumption.

### 3.1 Figure 21 · page 37

> **Figure 21.** *Annual cover floor for each conserved paddock's portion of a vegetation community, against the middle half of comparable grazed portions, by community. Water years 1988–89 to 2022–23. Flood years shaded. Common vertical scale across panels.*

**What the figure compares.** Each coloured line is one conserved paddock's portion of one vegetation community, year by year. The grey band covers the middle half of the grazed portions of that same community — a quarter sit above it and a quarter below — with the median drawn through it. A line inside the band is behaving like ordinary grazed country of the same kind. Both the lines and the band are measured on the same unit, the paddock-community part.

The unit is the part rather than the whole paddock, so a paddock appears in every community it holds. Bala 29ca holds all three in near-equal proportion and appears in all three panels. Bala 26ca and Bala 28ca each hold a Riverine portion alongside their Inland one and appear in both. Bala 27ca is entirely Inland Floodplain and appears once. **The Aeolian panel therefore carries a single conserved line, Bala 29ca's, and no comparison within that panel is a comparison between paddocks.**

**Two arms, not three.** The grey band is the 14-day rotational paddocks. The third management arm — the unzoned country that carries standard grazing — has no mapped boundary and is absent from this figure entirely. Figure 26 draws all three arms, and the unzoned arm sits at or above the rotational band in most strata. A reader taking this page alone will form a two-arm picture that Figure 26 corrects.

**What it shows.** In Inland Floodplain country the conserved parts other than Bala 29ca's sit inside the grazed band across the record. Bala 29ca's Inland part sits below it in several dry periods, notably the late 1990s, the mid-2000s and 2019. In Aeolian and Riverine country Bala 29ca's parts sit well below the band throughout, and rise toward it.

That rise begins in the early part of the record, roughly thirty years before conservation management commenced. Whatever explains it, the present grazing arrangement does not.

**What it does not show.** Nothing here separates the two management categories. That is a description of this figure, not a finding about grazing: the comparison rests on four conserved paddocks against sixty grazed ones, the conserved four are not distributed across the hydrological range, and no stocking record or cultivation history exists for any paddock. A design of this shape could not detect a grazing effect of moderate size, so its absence here is not evidence of absence.

**Limitation.** This contrast is grazed against conserved, not grazed against formerly-cropped, because cultivation history is unavailable.

### 3.2 Figure 22 · page 38

> **Figure 22.** *As Figure 21, with mean cover substituted for the cover floor. Same units, same panels, same scale convention.*

**A control, not a result.** On mean cover the separation collapses. In Aeolian country the difference between the conserved arm and the rotational comparator falls from −32.0 raw and −10.5 adjusted on the floor to −4.1 raw and −2.3 adjusted on the mean. Across the other two communities the conserved parts sit on or inside the band for most of the record.

**It does not vanish entirely, and the text must not say it does.** Bala 29ca's Aeolian line still runs below the band through the 1990s and 2000s before converging, and Bala 26ca's Riverine line still drops well below it in several years. What changes between Figures 21 and 22 is the size of the separation, not its existence: a difference of the order of thirty points becomes one of the order of four.

This is the argument of Section 5 appearing at unit scale. An assessment built on mean cover would report differences small enough to dismiss — not because there are none, but because averaging well-covered and poorly-covered ground within a unit removes most of the quantity that responds to water. Which figure gives the right answer depends on which quantity carries the ecological meaning, and Section 5 sets out why the poorest patches were chosen.

Read as a pair, Figures 21 and 22 make one point: the metric determines how much there is to see.

### 3.3 Figure 23 · page 39

> **Figure 23.** *Difference in cover floor between conserved and grazed country, per water year, as three series. Positive values indicate conserved above grazed. **Whole-paddock grain** — see note.*

**Note on grain.** Figures 21 and 22 compare paddock-community parts. This figure compares whole paddocks. The unit has changed, and the two are not numerically comparable.

**What it shows.** The series excluding Bala 29ca is flat: +0.057 percentage points per year, r = 0.22, mean gap −2.07 points, ranging from −7.04 to +4.99 across years and crossing zero in both directions. There is no movement toward or away from grazed country. Bala 29ca alone rises at +0.919 points per year, r = 0.85. The four-paddock series at +0.273, r = 0.77, is that one paddock carried by the average.

**Limitation.** A flat difference establishes that the difference is not changing. It does not establish that the difference is small, and no distance claim is made from this figure.

**Nomenclature.** "Three paddocks" here denotes Bala 26ca, 27ca and 28ca, excluding Bala 29ca as the outlier. A different three-paddock set exists in the results store, defined by monitoring-plot presence and excluding Bala 27ca. The two share only two members and the term is never used unqualified.

### 3.4 Figure 26 · page 42 — the grazing figure

> **Figure 26.** *Cover floor trajectories for three management arms against a fixed grazed comparator band, by community. Each panel carries the raw difference and the difference after within-stratum adjustment. [Stacked, per §2.]*

**The three arms.** Conserved — the four paddocks under no grazing. Rotational — sixty paddocks under 14-day rotation, drawn as the grey comparator band. Unzoned — property area carrying no management zone at all, which is standard grazing; it has no mapped boundary, so it is inferred from the absence of a rotational zone rather than confirmed. A plot-confirmed subset of the unzoned arm is drawn separately.

**Reading the two numbers.** The visible separation between a coloured line and the grey band is the raw difference. The labelled value is that difference after comparing each arm with the comparator separately within each wetness band and recombining weighted by stratum area. In Aeolian country a raw difference of −32.0 percentage points becomes −10.5 once like is compared with like. **Most of the apparent difference between management arms is hydrological position, not management.**

**The ordering runs opposite to the expected direction.** The conserved arm sits below the comparator in Aeolian and Riverine country, while both unzoned arms sit at or above it — the inferred arm in six of nine strata, the plot-confirmed subset in eight of nine. If heavier grazing reduced the cover floor, the ordering would be the reverse.

Two readings are available and the figure states both. Either grazing intensity does not order the cover floor on this property, or the unzoned land is in fact less grazed than the rotational land rather than more, which would invert the inferred ordering. **Nothing in the data distinguishes them, because no stocking record exists.** The arms are management categories, not measured intensities, and the conserved Aeolian panel is Bala 29ca alone.

**This is the figure behind the statement that management category does not order the results.** It is a statement about categories as recorded, not about grazing as a process.

## 4b. New items raised by the renders

**R-1 · Bala 26ca's Riverine line is drawn at full weight on about 2% of the paddock.** Bala 26ca is 98% Inland Floodplain. Its Riverine portion clears the 30-pixel support rule and therefore draws a line indistinguishable in weight from lines representing a third of a paddock. It is also the noisiest series on both Figure 21 and Figure 22, dropping to roughly 30% mean cover in years when the band sits near 60 — behaviour consistent with a small area rather than with a finding.

**This is the L-01 problem inverted:** L-01 warned against averaging unlike parts into one number; here a sliver is promoted to visual equality with a substantial one. Options, in order of preference: print each line's share of its paddock and its pixel count; weight line thickness by area; or raise the support rule above 30 pixels for this figure and state the cut. **A design-seat decision, not CC's.**

**R-2 · The Aeolian grey band appears to break around 2008–2010.** Confirm whether that is a data gap, a support-rule failure in those years, or a rendering artefact. If it is a genuine gap it must be stated; a band that silently stops is read as a band that continues.

**R-3 · Figure 26's Aeolian conserved panel is labelled `n=2` with a parenthetical `n=1: Bala 29ca`.** Two different counts in one label with no key. Report what each counts. Section 10 and the Figure 24 limitation both state the Aeolian conserved result rests on n = 1.

**R-4 · Two vocabularies, four pages apart.** Figures 21, 22, 26 and 27 are old-generation: they say "reference" and "not grazed", carry `veg_p05_spatial` as an axis label, and put four lines of explanation in the subtitle. Figure 23 is already remediated: plain-language title, "conserved", a question as the heading, explanation in the caption rather than the subtitle. **This is why pages 37, 38 and 42 read badly and page 39 does not.** The gap is generational, not compositional.

## 4c. Revised render priority — read this before authorising anything

**Figures 21 and 22 — stack, and move to portrait.** Three panels at ~6.7 in wide against ~3.0 in now. Horizontal resolution per water year roughly doubles. This is where the change budget buys something.

**Figures 26 and 27 — do not stack.** The layout is 3 arms × 3 communities. Fitting that grid into portrait gives panels about 2.2 in wide, which is *worse* than the 2.3 in they have now and worse than Figure 21 before any change. Stacking cannot help a 3 × 3 grid on one page. Either split into three figures, one per community — which is new work — or leave the layout alone.

**Recommendation: leave 26 and 27 as they are, and widen them to the full landscape text box (10.3 in) if anything is done at all.** Their result is the labelled adjusted-gap numbers, not the year-by-year trajectories, so compression costs less there than on Figure 21. This reverses the earlier instruction that 26 and 27 take the same treatment as 21 and 22.

What Figures 26 and 27 do need is the caption rewrite in §3.4 and the vocabulary change. Both are text.
